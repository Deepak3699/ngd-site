# How Much Does a Website Cost in India in 2026?

If you are planning a website for your business, you have probably received very different quotes. One developer may quote a few thousand rupees, while an agency may suggest a budget several times higher.

Both quotes can be reasonable—but they may not describe the same product.

A one-page website that displays your phone number is very different from an ecommerce store with payments, inventory, customer accounts and order tracking. The useful question is not simply **“What is the price of a website?”** It is **“What does my business need the website to do?”**

This guide explains the main website types, the factors that affect cost, the recurring expenses to plan for and how to compare proposals.

## Website development cost in India: a practical estimate

The ranges below are broad planning estimates for 2026, not fixed market rates or a quotation. Actual prices depend on scope, design quality, content, integrations, location and the experience of the team.

| Website type | Indicative build budget |
| --- | ---: |
| Basic landing page | ₹5,000–₹20,000 |
| Small business website (around 5–10 pages) | ₹15,000–₹60,000 |
| Custom business or corporate website | ₹40,000–₹1,50,000+ |
| Ecommerce website | ₹50,000–₹3,00,000+ |
| Custom web application or portal | ₹3,00,000+ |

These are deliberately wide ranges. Published Indian cost guides also show substantial variation between freelancers, small studios and larger agencies. For example, [Technscale's 2026 cost guide](https://technscale.com/blog/website-cost-india) separates basic business sites from custom lead-generation websites and ecommerce projects. [IfStatic's pricing guide](https://ifstatic.com/blog/website-development-cost-in-india) explains its estimates through project hours and assumed hourly rates.

Do not treat any online price table as a guaranteed quote. Use it to prepare a realistic brief and compare proposals with the same scope.

## What changes the price of a website?

### 1. Design: template or custom?

A ready-made theme can reduce design and development time. It may be suitable when you need a straightforward online presence and the template fits your brand.

Custom design takes more planning. It is useful when your business needs a distinctive identity, carefully designed service pages or a customer journey that a template cannot support well.

Custom does not automatically mean better. Pay for it when the design solves a real business or usability problem.

### 2. Features and integrations

A contact form is relatively simple. Features such as customer accounts, appointment booking, payment gateways, product filters, dashboards, inventory sync and CRM integrations require more development and testing.

Before asking for a quote, list what visitors and staff must be able to do. This is more useful than saying that you want a “modern website.”

### 3. Content, photography and branding

A website needs more than code. Someone must prepare the page copy, product descriptions, images, logo assets and calls to action.

If you provide complete, accurate content, the project may need less content-production work. If the developer or agency is expected to write copy, source images or create branding, include those tasks in the scope.

### 4. SEO and analytics

Basic technical SEO should be considered during development, not added as an afterthought. Discuss page titles, meta descriptions, heading structure, mobile usability, canonical URLs, sitemap, robots rules, structured data where relevant and analytics setup.

SEO implementation does not guarantee rankings. It gives search engines clearer information about your pages and helps you measure what happens after launch.

### 5. Maintenance and support

A website is not finished forever when it goes live. Software updates, backups, security checks, content changes, bug fixes and hosting support may all require ongoing work.

Ask what support is included after launch, how long it lasts and what additional work costs.

## Do not forget the recurring costs

The development quote is only one part of the budget. Depending on your setup, you may also pay for:

- **Domain renewal:** the yearly cost of keeping your website address.
- **Hosting or platform plan:** the service that keeps the website available.
- **Business email:** mailboxes using your own domain, if required.
- **Maintenance:** updates, backups, monitoring and technical support.
- **Third-party services:** payment gateway fees, premium plugins, booking tools or other integrations.

Check renewal prices rather than relying only on introductory offers. Ask who owns the domain, hosting account, source code and other paid subscriptions. Ideally, the business should control its own accounts and access.

## How to compare website development quotes

Ask every developer or agency to quote against the same checklist:

1. How many unique page layouts are included?
2. Is the design custom or based on a template?
3. Who supplies the text, images and other content?
4. Which features and integrations are included?
5. Is the website responsive and tested on mobile?
6. What SEO and analytics setup is included?
7. Who owns the domain, hosting account and source code?
8. How many revision rounds are included?
9. What is the delivery timeline and what do you need to provide?
10. What support is included after launch, and what does it cost later?

A low quote is not automatically a bargain if important features, ownership or support are missing. A high quote is not automatically justified either. Compare the deliverables, not just the final number.

## Which type of website does your business need?

- **New local business:** start with a clear landing page or compact business website containing services, location, contact details and a strong enquiry option.
- **Growing service company:** consider dedicated service pages, case studies, lead forms, analytics and a manageable content section.
- **Retail business:** choose an ecommerce platform or custom store based on catalogue size, payments, stock handling and fulfilment needs.
- **Business with a manual workflow:** if customers need logins, bookings, staff dashboards or custom processes, you may need a web application rather than a standard marketing website.

Start with the smallest version that solves the real problem. You can add features when customer demand and business operations justify them.

## Final thoughts

Website development in India does not have one standard price. The budget depends on what the website must achieve, how much custom work is required and what happens after launch.

Write down your goals, required pages, features and ongoing support needs before requesting quotes. A clear scope makes pricing easier to understand—and helps you avoid paying for features your business does not need.
