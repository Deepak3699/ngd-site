Search is changing from "ten blue links" to synthesized answers. When a user asks an AI search product a question, the system generates a response and may cite one or more pages as sources. The question for a business is no longer only **"how do I rank?"** — it is also **"will AI answer engines quote my site?"**

That is the focus of this guide: what Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) means, how it differs from Answer Engine Optimization (AEO), which practical tactics actually help, and what Google's own documentation says is and is not useful.

## What changed with search

Google's AI Overviews now appear on a large share of queries — by some trackers, 25% to 48% of all queries in 2026. Their impact on clicks is significant. Across eight studies reviewed by Driven Metrics, AI Overviews reduce organic click-through rates by around 40% on average, with position one losing roughly [57.7% of its click-through rate when an AI Overview appears](https://firstpagesage.com/seo-blog/ai-impact-on-website-traffic-report/). When an AI Overview is present, about [82.7% of searches end without a click](https://firstpagesage.com/seo-blog/ai-impact-on-website-traffic-report/), compared with about 62.9% without one.

That does not mean organic search is dead. Brands that are cited in AI Overviews earn roughly 35% more organic clicks than uncited brands, and visitors who do click tend to arrive [better prepared — spending longer on the site and converting more often](https://firstpagesage.com/seo-blog/ai-impact-on-website-traffic-report/). The practical shift is that "where my site appears" is now a layered question: search rankings, AI citations and brand recall.

## What is Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)?

GEO is a term used for the practice of optimizing content so that AI-powered answer systems can find it, understand it and quote it as a source. The concept comes from original research — the Aggarwal et al. study published in the [ACM SIGKDD '24 paper from Georgia Tech and Princeton](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735) — which measured how different content changes affect citation by generative engines.

Two findings matter most for a business:

1. **Citations, statistics and expert quotes help.** The study found that adding supporting sources, statistics and expert quotations each reliably increased how prominently a page was used in AI answers — by up to roughly 30–40% in its controlled experiments.
2. **Keyword stuffing does not work.** The same study found that stuffing keywords into AI-style queries produced almost no measurable lift and can actively hurt clarity.

GEO is therefore best understood as improving the quality and evidence density of a page — not as a set of tricks for gaming a specific AI model.

## GEO vs AEO: what is the difference?

The terms are used interchangeably in much of the industry. Google defines them explicitly in its May 2026 documentation:

- **AEO** — Answer Engine Optimization: optimizing so your content can be used as a direct answer in AI-powered answer engines.
- **GEO** — Generative Engine Optimization: the broader practice of optimizing for visibility and citation in generative AI search.

In [Google's own words](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/googles-new-ai-search-guide-calls-aeo-and-geo-still-seo/575026/): "From Google Search's perspective, optimizing for generative AI search is optimizing for the search experience, and thus still SEO." For Google's own generative features — AI Overviews, AI Mode and Gemini — AEO and GEO are still SEO, not separate disciplines.

## What AI answer engines actually look at

Different AI systems behave differently, but across [Google's AI-search documentation and third-party GEO guides](https://backlynk.io/blog/geo-aeo-generative-engine-optimization-2026-chatgpt-perplexity-claude/), the pattern is consistent:

- **Crawlability.** The page has to be reachable and indexable. If an AI crawler cannot access the page, it cannot quote it.
- **Clear, directly-answering content.** A user asks a question; the page answers it plainly, near the top, before adding depth. "Start with the answer" is the single highest-value habit.
- **Structured evidence.** Statistics with named, dated sources; quotations from authoritative sources; named entities and places; real dates and version numbers.
- **Source-date evidence.** Pages that state when the information was published or last updated look more trustworthy than anonymous pages.
- **Structured data.** FAQPage, Article, Organization and service schema improve machine readability and rich-result eligibility, and should match visible page text.
- **Consistent entity identity.** The same name, aliases, description and contact details should appear consistently across the site, in sitemap and in `llms.txt`.

## What Google says not to do (and what to do instead)

Google's May 2026 guidance lists tactics it explicitly does not need for its generative AI features. It includes:

- **llms.txt files** — Google says it does not use them. Other engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity) may use them as emerging signals, so a small, clean `llms.txt` is still a free hygiene step, but it is not a ranking shortcut.
- **Content chunking** — Google's models handle multi-topic pages without manual segmentation.
- **Rewriting content for AI** — synonyms and natural phrasing are recognized. Do not rewrite the same content "for AI".
- **Special structured data for AI** — Schema.org markup remains useful for rich results, but it is not a ticket to AI citations.
- **Inauthentic mentions and engineered citations** — these are explicitly rejected.

What to do instead: the boring fundamentals — people-first, non-commodity content; semantic HTML; accurate metadata; strong internal links; page experience and high-quality media.

## The llms.txt question: worth doing or not?

The `llms.txt` convention is a proposed Markdown file placed at your root that gives AI systems a compact, human-readable index of your most valuable pages, with one-line descriptions. It was [proposed by Jeremy Howard in September 2024](https://blog.starmorph.com/blog/aeo-geo-optimization-guide) and has been adopted by hundreds of thousands of sites.

The honest picture, supported by [Otterly.ai's three-month study](https://blog.starmorph.com/blog/aeo-geo-optimization-guide): no major AI provider has officially confirmed that its crawlers read or prioritize it, and only a tiny share of AI visits use it as a source. ChatGPT and Perplexity may treat it as an emerging signal; Google does not use it at all.

So — is it worth doing? Yes, as a free hygiene step: it takes minutes, costs nothing, and can help a user-directed agent find your right page quickly. It is **not** a ranking lever.

## A practical 2026 GEO checklist for a small business

1. **Answer the question in the first paragraph.** If a visitor asks "What does this cost?", answer it in the first screenful.
2. **Add statistics with named, dated sources.** "In 2026, 25% to 48% of Google queries showed AI Overviews (BrightEdge, 2026)" — specific, attributed, checkable.
3. **Quote experts and primary sources.** One short quote per meaningful claim beats ten unsourced sentences.
4. **Use clear, page-level identity.** A descriptive title, a one-sentence description, consistent entity name, and a "last updated" date.
5. **Add FAQ sections.** Answer real questions in plain language — FAQPage schema plus visible Q&A sections.
6. **Keep `llms.txt` and `llms-full.txt` clean.** A short index for discovery and a fuller guide for agents, both free.
7. **Link generously and internally.** Cross-link related guides, services and case pages — AI engines follow the same relevance logic as humans.
8. **Keep dates and numbers honest.** AI systems notice when a page is stale. If a statistic has changed, update it.
9. **Check crawlability and indexing.** Verify the page is indexable in Search Console, has the right canonical URL and no blocked robots rules.

## Does GEO actually drive traffic?

Currently, yes — [about 1.08% of all website traffic comes from AI platforms](https://paradigmmedianetworks.com/ai-search-statistics-2026/), rising to around 2.8% for IT sites, but the number is growing quickly. ChatGPT is responsible for roughly 87.4% of AI-referral traffic. What matters most is the quality of that traffic: B2B buyers are estimated to click through on AI Overview sources at rates close to 90%, compared with around 8% for general users, and AI-referred visitors convert at rates far higher than typical organic traffic.

If your business sells to professionals, a content page that answers their questions precisely may reach a small percentage of total visitors that converts at a disproportionate rate.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is GEO the same as SEO?

Partly. For Google's generative features, GEO is still SEO. For AI search products such as ChatGPT, Perplexity and Claude, though, it involves different systems and additional hygiene — such as `llms.txt` — that can help visibility.

### Does llms.txt guarantee citations?

No. No major AI provider has officially confirmed that its crawlers read it, and studies show it is used in only a small share of AI visits. It is a free, low-cost step worth doing, not a ranking shortcut.

### Can I get cited without being first in Google?

Yes. AI engines synthesize answers from multiple sources and may cite a page that does not rank first in traditional search. Clear, evidence-backed, directly-answering content is what helps in both worlds.

### Do I need to rewrite my site for AI?

No. Google explicitly says AI-specific rewrites are unnecessary. The better habit is to stay honest, keep pages updated, and make every page genuinely answer the question it targets.

## Final thoughts

GEO and AEO are not a separate discipline you bolt onto SEO. They are the same discipline with a sharper edge: answer well, cite well, be specific, keep your facts honest and make the page easy for both humans and machines to read.

The sites that win in this shift publish their evidence, keep their content fresh, and treat their audience as an expert who can check claims. That is good for Google, good for AI engines and — most importantly — good for the business.
